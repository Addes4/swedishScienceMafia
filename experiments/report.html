Let failures shape the next discovery.

Falsify turns an algorithm's regressions into executable counterexamples. A fixed evaluator checks each heuristic, an archive retains difficult inputs, and the search revisits them before accepting new ideas.

What the evidence supports: Counterexample replay reduced harmful drift in this experiment. The evolved policies did not beat best-fit overall. The repeated-run comparison uses stochastic mutations; the Codex-guided pilot is a separate exploratory experiment.

Does the tempting pilot improvement survive?

The revised heuristic won once and lost zero times on 1,000 exploratory cases. On 10,000 independent cases, it won 3 times, lost 14 times, and tied 9,983 times. Its average excess was 0.0011 bins.

The independent audit does not support a broad improvement claim.
