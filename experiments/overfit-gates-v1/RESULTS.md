# overfit-gates-v1: how much does selection overfit, and what do stricter promotion rules cost?

CPU-only study written on 4 October 2026, 01:48–03:05 BST. No API or Modal calls. Protocol:
[PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md), whose SHA-256 `9675d9f9…` was recorded in [RUN_LOG.md](RUN_LOG.md)
before any scoring. Every number below comes from [summary.json](summary.json) or
[tables.md](tables.md), both generated by `analyze.py`.

## Question and answer

**Question.**
1. When a closed LLM loop promotes on a small public suite, how much of the promoted program's
   advantage over best fit disappears on fresh instances?
2. If the same proposal streams go through stricter promotion rules, what do those rules gain and
   what do they cost?

**Data.** The 879 valid proposals from [memory-ablation-v1](../memory-ablation-v1/RESULTS.md)'s
confirmatory study: Claude Haiku 4.5 writing `priority` code for Weibull 5k bin packing, 30 runs
× 30 calls, score-only promotion on 5 fixed instances. Each proposal was re-scored on 40 fresh
instances, split into 24 for truth, 12 for a holdout and 4 for validation.

**Answer.**
- **Selection overfitting was small here.**
  - Each as-run promotion overstated its fresh advantage by **0.61 bins per instance** on average
    [0.02, 1.49] (17 promotions in 7 runs, runs resampled). 11 of the 17 had a positive gap.
  - The 7 promoted final incumbents averaged 10.31 bins per instance better than best fit on the
    fixed suite and 9.78 on fresh instances.
  - A public suite of 5 × 5,000 items is large enough that a 30-call search barely overfits it.
    The 0.960 → 0.238 collapse in tournament-v2 came from much smaller public sets.
- **Offline best-of-K is far below the textbook bound.**
  - Re-selecting among each run's proposals on random public subsets of the fresh pool, the gap
    grows with K and shrinks with n: at n = 1, from 0.03 (K = 1) to 0.53 (K = 30) bins per
    instance; at n = 10, from 0.01 to 0.10.
  - The bound σ·√(2 ln K / n), with σ ≈ 11–12 as the protocol defines it, predicts 10–32. It
    overstates the gap by 1–2 orders of magnitude.
  - Post hoc: the noise that actually separates competitive candidates (the SD of paired
    differences) has a median of only 0.87 bins per instance. Most proposals are far from the best
    or identical to it.
- **Strict veto gates threw away the largest real gains.**

  | Gate | Final fresh advantage over best fit, bins/instance [95% CI] | Change vs score-only |
  |---|---|---|
  | Score-only (as run) | 2.28 [0.19, 5.53] | — |
  | Strict counterexample-archive veto | 0.29 [−0.00, 0.72] | −1.99 [−5.22, 0.00], Holm p = 0.27 |
  | Random veto with inputs of the same length | 0.40 [0.00, 0.93] | −1.88 [−5.12, 0.00] |
  | Bounded-loss soft gate | 0.39 [−0.02, 0.95] | −1.89 [−5.15, 0.02] |

  - All three vetoes blocked the run with the largest gain: prose seed 9, +40.6 bins per instance
    fresh.
  - The archive veto and the random veto also blocked none seed 6, +15.7 bins per instance.
  - **The archive veto behaved like a random veto of the same input lengths:** false rejections
    differed by 0.07 per run [0.00, 0.20]. 554 of the 635 archived inputs are 2 items long. On a
    2-item stream, any policy that opens a new bin while an open bin fits loses, whether or not the
    input was a counterexample. The veto's blocking power came from input length, not from the
    inputs being counterexamples.
- **Statistical gates neither helped nor hurt the final score.**
  - The Ladder, the protocol's primary contrast, made 12 promotions instead of 17. Its final fresh
    advantage was the same: −0.0014 [−0.004, 0.0] bins per instance.
  - The sign test (5 wins and 0 losses out of 5) made 5 promotions with **0 false acceptances**,
    against 4 for score-only. It reached 2.23 against 2.28; the difference, −0.05 [−0.17, 0.03],
    is not significant.
  - Thresholdout matched score-only's final score at the cost of 52.7 million extra items packed.
    It made 45 false acceptances, 38 of them ties on the truth split. Its Laplace noise turns
    behavioural no-ops into "improvements", which is a poor property when no-ops are 65–82% of
    proposals.
- **Positive controls passed.**
  - Re-scoring reproduced the saved fixed-suite bins and packing hashes for 6 of 6 programs.
  - The score-only replay reproduced all 17 as-run promotions.
  - Deduplication was exact: 19 of 19 groups with two code texts agreed on every bin and hash.

The study is a replay (open loop), so the gates did not change what the model proposed next.
See Limitations.

### Design in brief

Full details are in PROTOCOL.md.
- Each gate keeps its own incumbent, starting at best fit, and sees the run's proposals in call
  order.
- **Truth:** a proposal is *truly better* if its mean bins on the 24 truth instances are lower
  than the gate's current incumbent's; *materially better* if lower by at least 0.5 bins per
  instance.
- **Archive:** the counterexamples mined in earlier calls of the same run.
- **Random veto:** the archive's inputs replaced by fresh random inputs of the same lengths.
- **Soft gate:** at most two archived inputs may regress, each by 1 bin, and the candidate must be
  no worse on 4 validation instances.

## Limitations

- **Open loop.**
  - In a live loop, a blocked candidate changes what the model sees next, so the veto gates'
    losses here are what they would have blocked, not what a live loop would have ended with.
  - The archive was mined against the as-run incumbent, not each gate's own.
- **One model, one problem, one public-suite size.** Selection overfitting depends strongly on the
  public set: 5 instances of 5,000 items here. Small or short public sets, as in the 80-item
  Falsify families or the one-instance AlphaEvolve problems, can behave very differently.
- **Few informative runs.** 23 of 30 runs never promoted, and the gate contrasts rest on 7 runs.
  Two of them (prose-s9 and none-s6) drive the veto results.
- **Small truth split.** 24 instances, so "truly better" is noisy below about 1 bin per instance.
  The material threshold rows are given for that reason.
- **Selection only.** The offline analysis measures selection overfitting, not adaptive
  overfitting. The proposals were generated from feedback on the original fixed suite.
- **Hand-set Thresholdout parameters** (T = 2, σ = 0.5). The ties problem follows from adding
  noise to exactly-zero differences and would remain at other σ.

## Reproduce

From the repository root, with the project venv:

```bash
python experiments/overfit-gates-v1/score.py control
python experiments/overfit-gates-v1/score.py pool --workers 2      # about 1 h on 2 workers
python experiments/overfit-gates-v1/score.py dedup --workers 2
python experiments/overfit-gates-v1/score.py archive --workers 2
python experiments/overfit-gates-v1/analyze.py                     # summary.json, tables.md
```

Scoring is deterministic: a re-run reproduces every bin count.

## Next steps

1. **Run the veto comparison live.** Archive veto, random veto and score-only in a closed loop, to
   see whether blocking changes later proposals.
2. **Test horizon-preserving counterexamples.** Use mid-stream windows of 500–1,000 items instead
   of shrunk 2-item inputs. This study suggests that the short length of the inputs, not their
   provenance, is what blocks long-horizon gains. That is research direction 5 in
   `docs/literature/research-directions.md` on branch `docs/research-directions`.
3. **Repeat the selection-gap measurement where overfitting can bite:** public sets of 1–2
   instances, or 80-item instances, and the AlphaEvolve problems with one public instance.
   Proposals would come from a cheap model with logged public and fresh scores.
4. **Consider the sign test as a default promotion rule.** In this replay it was the only gate
   with zero false acceptances that kept almost all of score-only's gain. Confirm it live before
   changing the loop's default.
