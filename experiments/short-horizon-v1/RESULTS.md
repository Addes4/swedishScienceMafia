# short-horizon-v1: short selection instances and short counterexample gates make an LLM loop myopic

**Question.** Do short selection instances, or short counterexample streams used as a gate, steer an LLM
search loop toward myopic online bin-packing rules that do worse on the 5,000-item streams the rule is
meant for?

**Answer: complete separation, though the pre-registered test cannot reach p < 0.05.** Every run
that selected on 5,000-item streams beat every run that selected on 200- or 80-item streams, by
about 2.3–2.5 pp on fresh 5,000-item instances. With 4 runs per arm, the pre-registered Holm-adjusted
test cannot go below p = 0.057 (see below), so we report complete separation at the design's
resolution, not significance at 0.05. The setup:
- The same one-command loop, the same model and the same 150 steps per run.
- The same number of items per selection evaluation.
- Only the length of the selection streams differs between arms.

The result:
- **Selection on 5,000-item streams (A):** the final rules beat best fit by 2.61 pp on 100 fresh
  5,000-item instances. Three of four runs reached −3.0 to −3.3 pp. FunSearch's heuristic is −3.31 pp
  and Sum-of-Squares −3.47 pp.
- **Selection on 200-item streams (B):** −0.33 pp.
- **Selection on 80-item streams (C):** −0.15 pp.

| Contrast | Mean | 95% CI | Exact permutation p | Holm p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B − A | +2.28 pp | [+1.19, +3.03] | 0.029 | 0.057 |
| C − A | +2.46 pp | [+1.40, +3.18] | 0.029 | 0.057 |

- Every A run beat every B and C run (complete separation).
- With 4 runs per arm, p = 2/70 = 0.029 is the smallest exact two-sided p possible, and Holm over two
  contrasts cannot go below 0.057. The pre-registered design could not reach Holm p < 0.05, which we
  did not check before running. We report the contrasts as complete separation at the design's
  resolution, not as significant at 0.05.

**Mechanism.** The short-trained rules almost never open a new bin while an open one has room:

| Policy | Share of placements that open a new bin while one fits |
|---|---|
| A (5,000-item selection) | 0.169 |
| B (200-item selection) | 0.020 |
| C (80-item selection) | 0.014 |
| FunSearch's heuristic | 0.347 |
| Sum-of-Squares | 0.310 |

Opening a bin early is exactly the move that loses on short streams and wins on long ones.

**Short-stream gates.** Adding a short-stream veto to 5,000-item selection removed most of the gain:

| Arm | Δ vs best fit (pp) | Contrast with A |
|---|---|---|
| D: suite of 519 mined counterexamples (438 are 2 items long) | −0.58 | D − A = +2.03 [+0.42, +3.18] |
| E: random streams of the same lengths | −0.09 | E − A = +2.52 [+1.46, +3.18] |

- D − E = −0.49 [−1.66, +0.18], p = 0.63: no evidence that the counterexamples' content matters
  beyond their short length.
- This is the live confirmation of the replay result in overfit-gates-v1.

**Cost and load.**
- $1.84 of Hugging Face credit (20 runs × 150 DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash calls); cap $4.00.
- Runs: 05:46–08:27 BST, 4 October 2026. Fresh audit: 08:49–09:03.
- The machine was heavily loaded by other sessions (1-minute load up to about 110 on 10 cores).
  Timeouts were rare and fell on the control arm, A (below). If anything, load biased the primary
  contrasts toward zero.

How the columns are defined:
- **Myopia:** the share of placements on the first 10 audit instances that open a new bin while an
  open bin has room.
- **τ:** Kendall's τ, per run, between each valid candidate's selection score and its hidden score
  (2 × 5,000 items, never used for selection). The table gives the arm mean.
- **Long-horizon false rejections:** candidates rejected (not better, or gate-rejected) whose hidden
  score beat the current incumbent's.
- **Material:** by more than 5e-4 of the normalised score, about one bin per instance. This threshold
  was added in `analyze.py` and was not stated in the protocol.
- **False acceptances:** promotions whose hidden score fell below the previous incumbent's.

### Generation or selection? (post hoc)

Every candidate also has a hidden score on 2 × 5,000 items. `best_available.json` compares the best
hidden score any candidate reached with the final incumbent's:

| Arm | Final incumbent's hidden score, per run | Best candidate's hidden score, per run | Candidates with hidden ≥ 0.985, per run |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.980, 0.971, 0.993, 0.989 | 0.993, 0.972, 0.994, 0.993 | 18, 0, 69, 46 |
| B | 0.967, 0.968, 0.963, 0.964 | 0.970, 0.971, 0.990, 0.978 | 0, 0, 1, 0 |
| C | 0.963, 0.963, 0.968, 0.963 | 0.990, 0.970, 0.988, 0.985 | 2, 0, 2, 0 |
| D | 0.963, 0.985, 0.963, 0.963 | 0.974, 0.986, 0.965, 0.965 | 0, 1, 0, 0 |
| E | 0.966, 0.963, 0.963, 0.963 | 0.969, 0.991, 0.990, 0.970 | 0, 3, 1, 0 |

FunSearch's heuristic scores 0.994 on these two hidden instances, and best fit 0.963.

In B, C and E the model did write some strong long-horizon rules; the best reached 0.990 against
FunSearch's 0.994. The short-stream score or the short-stream veto rejected them. In A the good rules
were promoted, and later proposals built on them: 18 to 69 strong candidates per run. Both effects
are visible:
- Short selection discards the occasional good long-horizon rule.
- Without such a promotion, the search never compounds.

The design cannot separate how much of the gap comes from what the model proposed and how much from
what the loop selected. The feedback labels truthfully name the stream lengths, and they differ
between arms.

## What was done

**Arms.** Five arms of `python -m autoresearch.loop` with its defaults (lean loop, non-regression gate,
integrity gate):
- `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash:deepinfra` on the Hugging Face router;
- 150 steps per run, a $0.20 cap per run, 1 gate worker, 4 seeds;
- 4 runs at a time.

The problem folders `problems/bp_sh_*` are generated by `make_problems.py`:
- They share `problem.md`, which states the rule will be used on 5,000-item streams, the initial
  program (best fit), the hidden instances and the item generator.
- In multi-stream instances the trusted driver re-executes the candidate module for every stream, so
  state starts fresh per stream.
- The D and E suites score 1 − 1e-8 × (streams where the rule uses more bins than best fit), so they
  act almost only through the loop's non-regression gate.

## Limitations

- **Few runs.** With 4 runs per arm, Holm-adjusted p cannot go below 0.057, which we did not check
  before running. The separation is complete, but the run-to-run variance comes mostly from the model
  and is not reproducible from the seed.
- **Generation and selection are confounded.** The feedback labels name the stream lengths, and the
  score scale differs between arms (0.93–0.95 on short streams, 0.96 on long ones). The model may have
  proposed differently as well as being selected differently.
- **One model, one budget, one family.** A single cheap model at 150 steps, on one distribution
  (Weibull). Bin packing makes the short-horizon conflict especially sharp. Opening a new bin can
  never pay on a 2-item stream, so a general claim needs a second domain.
- **Fixed suites.** D's counterexamples were mined in memory-ablation-v1 from Claude Haiku runs
  against a different incumbent, not live from these runs. E's random streams have the same lengths.
- **Public seeds differ between arms.** The fresh audit, the hidden instances and the generator are
  shared.
- **Load.** Heavy machine load from other sessions caused 3 timeout-invalid or partially timed-out
  evaluations in arms A and D (table above), and probably one lost promotion in arm A.

## Next steps

1. **More runs, for Holm-significant primary contrasts.** 6–8 runs per arm would let Holm reach
   p < 0.05. That is about $0.09 per run, roughly $1–2 more.
2. **Separate generation from selection.** Show every arm identical feedback (for example, a
   normalised score only) while selecting on different lengths. Or replay arm A's proposal streams
   under short-stream selection.
3. **Test the proposed fix: horizon-preserving counterexamples.** Use failing windows of 500–1,000
   items cut from 5,000-item streams, or shrinking that keeps length, against 2-item shrunk inputs.
4. **Find a second domain with a short/long conflict,** such as online scheduling with long-run
   throughput or TSP construction heuristics at different sizes, to test generality.
5. **Feed this back into the loop's defaults.** The one-command loop's non-regression gate is harmless
   on 5,000-item instances, but a Falsify-style short-counterexample archive should not be used as a
   promotion gate on long-horizon problems. `autoresearch/LOOP.md` should say so.
