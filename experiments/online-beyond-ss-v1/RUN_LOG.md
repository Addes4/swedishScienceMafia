| 02:18 | **Prior art for the weighting (read in full text):** Csirik et al. 2006 (arXiv cs/0210013), §8.1, "Objective functions that take level into account". They study Σ_h N(h)² f(h,B), where h is the bin *level* (fill), so gap = B − h. Their examples are N(h)²(B−h), [N(h)(B−h)]² and N(h)²/h (D. Wilson, 1996), all weighting *large* gaps more. Theorem 8.1: every such f keeps the Square Root and Bounded Waste properties. Their experiments on U{h,100} and U{18,j,100} found "no clear winner". So a weighted SS is a known family, and our weights (heavier on *small*, hard-to-fill gaps, from the learned CDF) are a member that has the same guarantees. The new part to verify is the *combination* with a best-fit finish and the benchmark result. Bender et al., JEA 2007 (SS variants for speed and memory allocation) have no such weighting or finish. |
