# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The 1.13731 plateau is now confirmed as a *seed-family-independent* ceiling spanning Gen 10–15, 18, 19, and the entire Gen 20–29 batch.** Gen 20 (`reflect_aggressive_sa`), Gen 21 (`difference_set_sidon_solver`), Gen 22 (`budget_adaptive_sa_golomb`), Gen 24 (`sum_set_compaction_move`), Gen 26 (`ratio_aware_topk_seed_selection`), Gen 27 (`iterative_growth_with_weight_sweep`), Gen 28 (`spectral_sidon_anneal`), and Gen 29 (`targeted_sum_compaction`) all land at exactly 1.13731. These span reflection-heavy SA, Singer difference sets, Bose-Chowla quadratic sets, greedy Golomb, dense-core hybrids, and sum-set compaction — yet none exceeds the plateau. The ceiling is structural to the seed+local-search paradigm, not to any particular construction.

- **Dense-core + Golomb tail remains the reliable route to the plateau (Gen 20, 22, 24).** Gen 22's explicit `(m, gs, gap, stride, scale)` sweep with budget-adaptive temperature scaling reproduces exactly the same value as Gen 19's simpler `(m, gs, gap)` sweep. Gen 24's `sum_set_compaction_move` adds targeted |A+A| reduction on top of the same seed and still lands at 1.13731. Adding SA temperature scheduling, watchdog restarts, or compaction moves to the dense-core+Golomb family does not shift the ceiling — confirming the seed basin, not the polish, is binding.

- **Directly attacking the denominator (|A+A|) via compaction does not break the plateau (Gen 24, 25, 29).** Gen 25 (`sumset_compaction_sidon`) minimizes |A+A| subject to a |A−A| floor and actually *regresses* to 1.13318. Gen 24 and Gen 29 apply similar compaction with floor=0.97 and remain at 1.13731. The current best program (Gen 29) embeds `_sum_compaction_step` with `window=25, floor=0.97` and a 40% probability of triggering — yet the score is unchanged. This is strong evidence that the ratio objective is *already near its local optimum* under Sidon-like constraints, and that compaction moves merely trade sum-set size for difference-set size without net gain.

- **The current best program's multi-phase structure (algebraic seeds → bounded Sidon sweep → greedy Sidon → weighted growth search → compaction+perturb local search → reflection polish) is robust but not superior.** Its `_growth_search` with weight sweep `[0.15, 0.25, 0.35, 0.5]` and `max_stall=3` is a more elaborate version of Gen 19's `_grow_by_difference` (single weight 0.35), yet both yield 1.13731. The additional complexity buys robustness across seeds, not score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Over-budget internal deadlines cause categorical failure (Gen 23, score 0.00).** Gen 23 (`adaptive_sa_golomb_restart`) timed out at 120s despite a 110s internal deadline — the seed-pool construction (Bose-Chowla + greedy/bounded Sidon + dense-Golomb) plus 20s watchdog restarts pushed past the harness limit, and no `sum-difference` metric was produced. This replicates Gen 16/17's failure mode from the prior batch: correct logic + over-budget search = 0.00. The current best avoids this by capping local search at 100s and growth at `deadline - 30`, but the margin is thin.

- **Compaction with a hard difference floor regresses the score (Gen 25, 1.13318 < 1.13731).** Gen 25's `sumset_compaction_sidon` uses a deterministic replacement scan minimizing |A+A| subject to a |A−A| floor. The floor constraint (analogous to floor=0.97 in the current best) forces the search to sacrifice difference-set size for sum-set size, and the net ratio *drops* below the plateau. This is the only Gen 20–29 program to score below 1.13731, and it directly demonstrates that aggressive sum-compaction is counterproductive when it erodes |A−A|.

- **Pure log-ratio seed ranking without size stabilization risks degenerate cardinality (Gen 26).** Gen 26's "ratio-aware top-K seed collector ranks candidates by pure log-ratio before refining." While it still hits 1.13731, the prior Gen 17 insight that "pure log-ratio score with no size bonus risks degenerate cardinality choices" remains relevant — Gen 26's ranking ignores the `(1 − 1/n)/100` bonus that the current best retains, and its plateau-level result suggests the bonus is a necessary stabilizer rather than an optimization lever.

- **Reflection polish remains a no-op for score (Gen 20, 25, 28).** Gen 20 ("reflection hill-climbing reflecting top/bottom/mid fractions about many candidate centers"), Gen 25 ("reflection polishing"), and Gen 28 ("reflection/block-shift polishing") all apply multi-center reflection and all land at ≤1.13731. The prior insight that reflection is "already implicitly captured by the dense-core+Golomb construction" is now reinforced across four additional generations.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best's `_sum_compaction_step` uses an O(n²) rebuild of `sums_no_i` and `diffs_no_i` per call, which is the same inefficiency flagged in Gen 24.** Gen 24's feedback explicitly notes that "`_sets_after_change` helper rebuilds sets from scratch (O(n²)), limiting refinement efficiency." The current best's `_sum_compaction_step` has the identical pattern: it precomputes `sums_no_i = {x + y for x in others for y in others}` and `diffs_no_i = {x - y for x in others for y in others}` from scratch on every invocation. Since this function is called at 40% probability inside the local-search loop, it dominates iteration cost — yet the plateau value is unchanged, confirming that *more iterations on this move set do not help*.

- **The current best's `_growth_search` accepts non-improving moves via `new_score >= best_score - eps` with `eps=0.001`, which is larger than the entire inter-generation spread (~0.0013).** This tolerance allows the search to drift away from the best-scoring configuration while still updating `best` only on strict improvement. Combined with `max_stall=3`, the growth phase has limited explorative reach — it is effectively a bounded greedy extension, matching Gen 19's `_grow_by_difference` behavior. The weight sweep `[0.15, 0.25, 0.35, 0.5]` is the only novel element versus Gen 19, and it yields no measurable gain.

- **The `_bounded_sidon` center-bias heuristic (`key=lambda x: (abs(x - center), rng.random())`) is the current best's most direct sum-set-compaction mechanism at seed time.** By preferring candidates near the span midpoint, it keeps |A+A| small by construction — the same motivation as Gen 25's compaction, but applied at construction rather than as a post-hoc move. Both approaches plateau, suggesting the center-bias already saturates the achievable sum-set compaction for Sidon-like sets.

- **The current best retains the `(1 − 1/n)/100` size bonus in `_score`, matching Gen 19 and diverging from Gen 26's pure-ratio ranking.** This bonus is ≈0.0099 at n=100 and ≈0.00997 at n=1000 — comparable to the entire inter-generation spread. Its retention is a deliberate anti-degeneracy stabilizer, not a score contributor, consistent with the prior insight that it "only prevents collapse to tiny sets."

## Performance Analysis

- **The 1.13731 plateau now spans nineteen generations (Gen 10–15, 18–22, 24, 26–29), with only two sub-plateau results in the entire recent batch: Gen 25 (1.13318) and Gen 23 (0.00).** The total spread across all successful programs remains ~0.0013 (≈0.1% of best-known). No Gen 20–29 program closes any of the ~0.1442 gap to best-known (1.28155). This is now overwhelmingly strong evidence that the best-known value requires a qualitatively different construction — likely non-Sidon-like sets that deliberately trade difference collisions for larger sum collisions, as previously hypothesized.

- **Gen 25's regression to 1.13318 is the only score *decrease* in the batch, and it comes from the most aggressive sum-compaction approach.** This is a critical negative result: directly minimizing |A+A| subject to a |A−A| floor *hurts* the ratio. The current best (Gen 29) uses a softer version (floor=0.97, window=25, probabilistic trigger) and remains at plateau — showing that the floor magnitude and move probability are finely balanced, and that harder compaction tips into regression.

- **Gen 23's 0.00 timeout is the only categorical failure in the batch, and it is a budget-management failure, not an algorithmic one.** Its logic (Bose-Chowla + greedy/bounded Sidon + dense-Golomb + SA with watchdog restarts) is sound; the 110s internal deadline plus seed-pool construction exceeded the 120s harness limit. This mirrors Gen 16/17's prior failure and reinforces that *phase budgeting is as important as move design* — the current best's `deadline - 30` guard for growth search and 100s local-search cap are the concrete pattern that avoids this.

- **The current best's score of 1.13731 equals every other successful Gen 20–29 program except Gen 25.** Its elaborate multi-phase pipeline (algebraic seeds → bounded Sidon → greedy Sidon → weighted growth → compaction+perturb → reflection) provides no score advantage over Gen 19's simpler dense-core+Golomb sweep. The value of the current best is *robustness across seed families*, not exceeding the plateau — consistent with the prior conclusion that "the bottleneck is move design, not iteration count," now extended to "the bottleneck is seed structure, not refinement objective."

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the two independent `deadline = time.time() + 100` blocks with a single shared wall-clock budget and a hard `time.time() < deadline` guard inside every inner loop.** The current best sets `deadline` twice (once before growth search, once before local search), so the total runtime is up to 100s (growth, capped at `deadline-30`) + 100s (local search) + seed-pool construction + final polish — exactly the over-budget pattern that produced Gen 23's 0.00 timeout. Set `t0 = time.time()` once at the top of `solve()`, define `DEADLINE = t0 + 105` (leaving a 15s harness margin), and pass `DEADLINE` into `_growth_search` and the local-search loop, checking `time.time() < DEADLINE` before each iteration rather than only at the outer level. This directly addresses the only categorical failure mode in the batch and is required before any additional exploration can be safely added.

2. **Replace the 40s exhaustive Phase-1 sweep (`primes` up to 200 × shifts × reflections, plus `_bounded_sidon` over 7 sizes × 4 span_mults × 3 seeds, plus `_greedy_sidon`) with a ratio-aware top-K seed collector.** Gen 26 showed that pure log-ratio ranking without a size bonus reaches the same plateau, so use pure `log|A−A|/log|A+A|` as the ranking key, retain the `(1−1/n)/100` bonus only as a tiebreaker, and keep the top K=20 seeds rather than the single best. Feed these K seeds into `_growth_search` in descending-ratio order, so refinement starts from the highest-ratio basin instead of the first improving config — directly exploiting the insight that "the value is in starting refinement from the highest-ratio seed." This also frees roughly 30s of the budget for the more promising refinement phases.

3. **Add a *diversity-preserving* seed augmentation: after top-K selection, generate 3–5 seeds by taking the best seed and applying `_reflect_half` at multiple centers/fractions, then re-ranking by pure log-ratio.** The global insights confirm reflection is a no-op for *score* (Gen 20, 25, 28) but is cardinality- and roughly difference-preserving, so reflected variants produce structurally distinct seeds with nearly the same |A−A|. Feeding these into `_growth_search` provides genuine basin diversity at near-zero cost, addressing the observation that "the bottleneck is seed structure, not refinement objective" — the reflected seeds are seed-structure perturbations that the current best's single-seed refinement cannot reach. Apply the reflection at centers `{0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7}` of the span and fractions `{0.3, 0.4, 0.5}` of the top marks, keeping only distinct-cardinality outputs.

4. **Make `_sum_compaction_step` incremental by maintaining `sums_no_i` and `diffs_no_i` from a cached multiset, rather than rebuilding them O(n²) on every call.** Gen 24's feedback already flagged the O(n²) `_sets_after_change` rebuild, and the current best has the identical pattern: `sums_no_i = {x + y for x in others for y in others}` is recomputed from scratch each invocation at 40% trigger probability. Cache `base_sums` and `base_diffs` at the top of the local-search loop and update them incrementally when `cur` changes (remove contributions of the replaced element, add the new one); this makes each compaction step O(n) instead of O(n²), enabling far more compaction attempts within the same budget. Because the plateau is unchanged by more compaction iterations, the *value* of this change is that it frees budget for recommendations 1–3 — but it is a prerequisite for any of them to fit.

5. **Replace the fixed `T=0.08` implicit temperature and `random.random() < 0.03` non-improving acceptance with a budget-adaptive schedule, and add a no-improvement watchdog that restarts from a fresh top-K seed.** Compute `T = 0.15 * (remaining/105) + 0.005` and raise the non-improving acceptance floor to ~0.10 during the first 30s, decaying to ~0.02 late — directly addressing Gen 3's observation that low acceptance (~0.02–0.03) restricts exploration. Combine with a 20s no-improvement watchdog (matching Gen 23's restart mechanism but under the shared budget from rec 1) that triggers a fresh `_bounded_sidon` or `_reflect_half` seed from the top-K pool rather than a random restart, so the watchdog explores seed-structure diversity rather than re-entering the same basin. This targets the "plateau-with-diverse-seeds" pattern where richer seeding alone failed, by pairing richer seeding with explicit exploration pressure.
