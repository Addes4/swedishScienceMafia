# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Structured dense-core + Golomb seeding remains the reliable route to the 1.13731 plateau.** The current best (`golomb_dense_crossover`, Gen 19) explicitly combines a dense interval `{0..m}` with a greedy near-Golomb block offset by a gap, sweeping `m ∈ [0,16)`, `gs ∈ [2,18)`, and `gap ∈ {1,2,3,5,8,13,21,34}`. This structured sweep reproduces the same 1.13731 achieved by Gen 10 (`hybrid_golomb_sa_v2`), Gen 11 (`add_bounded_sidon_and_better_local_search`), Gen 12 (`simanneal_restart_sidon`), Gen 13 (`multi_reflection_polish`), Gen 14, Gen 15 (`sidon_reflect_crossover`), and Gen 18 (`multi_cardinality_search`). The consistency across Golomb-based, Sidon-based, and reflection-based seed families confirms the plateau is a *seed-family-independent* ceiling.

- **Layering a difference-maximizing greedy growth phase on top of SA is unique to the current best and does not degrade the plateau.** Gen 19's `_grow_by_difference` explicitly scores candidate additions by `d_gain − 0.35 * s_cost`, directly biasing toward new differences at low sum cost. This is the only evaluated program with an explicit asymmetric gain/cost tradeoff in the growth phase; it still lands at 1.13731, showing that this targeted heuristic is *safe* but not *sufficient* to break the ceiling.

- **Multi-center reflection as a polish operator (Gen 13, 14, 15) is a valid difference-preserving move but yields no measurable gain.** Gen 13's `multi_reflection_polish` and Gen 15's `sidon_reflect_crossover` both exploit the fact that reflecting top marks through a quantile center shrinks |A+A| while approximately preserving the difference multiset. Despite being structurally clever, both land at exactly 1.13731 — the same as Gen 19 which lacks reflection. This suggests reflection is *already implicitly captured* by the dense-core+Golomb construction.

- **Diverse seed portfolios (Gen 8 previously, Gen 19 now) reach the plateau robustly.** The best program's Phase 1 sweep over 16×16×8 ≈ 2048 structured configs plus pure dense intervals for `n ∈ [2,80)` provides broad coverage; combined with Phase 3 restarts from randomized `(m, gs, gap)` triples, it finds the plateau reliably rather than by luck.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Fixed internal deadlines that exceed the external budget cause total failure (score 0.00).** Gen 16 (`relaxed_sidon_diff_collisions`) and Gen 17 (`multi_cardinality_search`) both timed out at 120s because they allocated ~100s of internal search on top of nested loops over primes, sizes, spans, seeds, and diff budgets. This is a categorical failure mode distinct from plateauing: correct logic + over-budget search = 0.00. The current best avoids this by capping Phase 1 at 40s and using a 110s total deadline with explicit `remaining < 3` guards before sub-searches.

- **Pure log-ratio scoring without a size bonus risks degenerate cardinality choices (Gen 17).** Gen 17's feedback explicitly flags that "the pure log-ratio score with no size bonus also risks degenerate cardinality choices." The current best retains the `(1 - 1/n)/100` bonus, which forces the search toward non-trivial set sizes — a small but important stabilizer.

- **Difference-maximization alone (Gen 5's pure Golomb rulers) regresses to 1.13626.** This remains the only sub-plateau result and confirms that maximizing |A−A| in isolation (n(n−1)+1 for true Golomb) inflates |A+A| too much. The current best's balanced dense-core+Golomb construction avoids this by construction.

- **Single-element perturbation with low non-improving acceptance (Gen 3, and implicitly the current best) fails to escape the plateau.** Gen 19's `_refine` uses `T = 0.08` decaying at 0.997 every 500 steps with a mix of shift/add/drop/swap — essentially the same move set as Gen 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, all of which plateau at 1.13731. The move *design*, not the temperature schedule, is the binding constraint.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best's `_incremental_score` exploits symmetry (i ≤ j) to halve the difference/sum computation.** This is a concrete efficiency win over `_score` (full double loop), enabling more SA iterations within the same 110s budget. However, since Gen 19's score equals Gen 10–15's, this speedup did not translate into a score gain — confirming the previous insight that "additional search time on the current move set yields no improvement."

- **`_grow_by_difference` uses a hard revert on non-improvement (`cur = best[:]` then `break`).** This makes Phase 4 a one-shot greedy extension from the current best rather than an iterative explorative phase. Combined with the 0.35 sum-cost weight, it is a conservative addition that cannot hurt the plateau but also cannot climb past it.

- **The scoring bonus `(1 - 1/n)/100` remains nearly negligible (≈0.0099 at n=100, ≈0.00997 at n=1000).** As noted previously, this is comparable to the entire inter-generation spread (0.00133). The current best's use of this bonus does not materially change its ranking against the plateau; it only prevents collapse to tiny sets.

- **Phase 1's config sweep is exhaustive in `(m, gs, gap)` but bounded to `m < 16`, `gs < 18`.** The dense core is capped at 16 elements and the Golomb block at 17, giving a maximum seed size of ~33. Yet the plateau is reached by every generation regardless of seed size — reinforcing that the ceiling is structural, not size-limited.

- **Phase 3 restarts use sub-deadlines (`min(remaining, 12)`) per restart, ensuring diversity of exploration before the final polish.** This is a robustness improvement over Gen 16/17's monolithic budget, and it correctly avoids the timeout failure mode.

## Performance Analysis

- **The 1.13731 plateau now spans eleven generations (Gen 10–15, 18, 19) plus the prior Gen 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8.** The total spread across *all* successful programs remains 1.13598–1.13731 (≈0.1% of best-known). The current best is a *tie* for the plateau, not an improvement — its value is in robustness across seed families, not in exceeding prior results.

- **The gap to best-known (1.28155) is stable at ~0.1442 and unchanged by any new generation.** No evaluated program closes more than 1% of this gap, and the addition of Gen 18/19's multi-cardinality and difference-maximizing growth phases did not shift the ceiling. This strongly supports the prior conclusion that the best-known value requires a *qualitatively different construction* — likely non-Sidon-like sets that deliberately trade difference collisions for larger sum collisions, as previously hypothesized.

- **Two failure modes are now clearly distinguished: timeout (0.00) and plateau (1.13731).** Gen 16 and 17's 0.00 scores are not algorithmic failures but budget-management failures — their logic is "correct" but unexecutable within 120s. This contrasts with Gen 19, whose phased deadlines (40s sweep, 110s total, 12s sub-restarts) ensure valid output.

- **The current best's score of 1.13731 is achieved with a *simpler* move set than Gen 13/14/15's reflection polish.** Since all reach the same value, reflection's incremental complexity is not justified by performance — a concrete instance of the prior insight that "the bottleneck is move design, not iteration count."

- **Generation 19's `_grow_by_difference` with `gain = d_gain − 0.35 * s_cost` is the closest any evaluated program comes to explicitly modeling the ratio objective's asymmetric tradeoff.** Its failure to improve the score is strong evidence that the plateau is imposed by the *seed structure* (dense core + Golomb always yields ~1.137), not by the refinement objective — because even a refinement phase that directly optimizes the tradeoff cannot escape the basin of the seeds it starts from.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace Phase 1's fixed `(m, gs, gap)` sweep with a ratio-aware seed selector that ranks dense+Golomb configs by the pure log-ratio (no size bonus) before refinement.** Gen 19 spends 40s exhaustively enumerating ~2048 configs but only keeps the single best seed; instead, collect the top-K (e.g., K=20) seeds by pure `log|A−A|/log|A+A|` and refine them *in parallel order* within the 110s budget. Since the plateau is seed-family-independent, the value is in starting refinement from the *highest-ratio* seed rather than the first improving one, and top-K diversity guards against local basins. Keep the `(1-1/n)/100` bonus only as a tiebreaker, not the primary ranking key.

2. **Extend `_grow_by_difference` to be iterative with a soft revert instead of a hard `break` on non-improvement.** Currently the growth phase is one-shot: on the first non-improving addition it reverts to `best` and breaks, so it cannot explore multi-step growth paths. Change it to accept additions that keep `cur_score >= best_score - ε` (e.g., ε=0.001) for up to 3 consecutive steps before reverting, and sweep the tradeoff weight `w ∈ {0.15, 0.25, 0.35, 0.5}` in `gain = d_gain − w * s_cost` across restarts. This makes the only explicit tradeoff-modeling phase in the best program actually explorative rather than a single greedy probe, directly targeting the observation that "even a refinement phase that optimizes the tradeoff cannot escape the basin of the seeds it starts from."

3. **Add a targeted sum-set compaction move to `_refine` that scans replacement values for one element to minimize |A+A| subject to a |A−A| floor.** The current move set only perturbs single elements randomly, which prior feedback flags as the binding constraint. Implement: pick a random index `i`, compute `base_diffs` and `base_sums`, then for candidate values `v` in a small window around `a[i]` (say ±30) evaluate `new_sums = |base_sums ∪ {v+z, v-z}|` and `new_diffs`, accepting the `v` minimizing `|new_sums|` subject to `|new_diffs| >= 0.95 * |base_diffs|`. This directly attacks the denominator of the log-ratio rather than hoping random walks hit it, and is much cheaper than `_grow_by_difference`'s full recomputation.

4. **Generalize the reflection idea into a multi-center, multi-fraction polish operator applied after Phase 3 restarts.** Gen 13/14/15 showed reflection preserves differences while shrinking sums but was only applied at the midpoint; generalize by enumerating centers `c ∈ {quantiles 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7}` of the current best and fractions `f ∈ {0.3, 0.4, 0.5}` of the top marks, evaluating each reflected variant with `_incremental_score`, and greedily applying the best. Since reflection is cardinality-preserving and roughly difference-preserving, this is a cheap structural move that can be applied repeatedly (loop until no reflection improves) without the risk of the hard-revert break that limits `_grow_by_difference`.

5. **Make the SA temperature schedule and non-improving acceptance explicitly budget-adaptive rather than fixed at `T=0.08` decaying at 0.997.** Prior feedback (Gen 3) blamed low acceptance (~0.02–0.03) for restricted exploration, yet Gen 19 still uses an implicit low acceptance via the small `T`. Compute `T` from remaining budget: `T = 0.15 * (remaining/110) + 0.005`, so early refinement explores broadly and late refinement exploits, and raise the non-improving acceptance floor to ~0.10 during the first 30s. Combine with a 20s no-improvement watchdog that triggers a fresh `_build_dense_golomb` restart with a new random seed, directly addressing the "plateau-with-diverse-seeds" pattern where richer seeding alone failed to break 1.137.
