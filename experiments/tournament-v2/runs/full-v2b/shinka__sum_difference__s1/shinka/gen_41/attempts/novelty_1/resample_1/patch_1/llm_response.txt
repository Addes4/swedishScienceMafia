<DESCRIPTION>
The current program has two structural weaknesses that limit its score:

1. **No hard wall-clock budget**: `solve()` sets `deadline = time.time() + 108` but then Phase 1 uses a *separate* `phase1_end = time.time() + 22`, and Phase 2/3 rely on relative offsets from `deadline`. Since the harness gives ~120s total and Phase 1's nested loops over `m, gs, gap, stride, scale` can be very slow (1400+ `_build_dense_golomb` calls each doing ~50 random candidate evaluations), the seed-bank construction can easily blow the budget before Phase 2 even starts. I unify everything to a single `t0 = time.time()` / `HARD_DEADLINE = t0 + 105` (leaving a ~15s harness margin), guard every inner loop with `time.time() < HARD_DEADLINE`, and shrink the dense-Golomb sweep so it can't dominate.

2. **Seed ranking ignores the actual objective**: the seed bank is ranked by `pure` ratio only, but the score we are maximizing is `full = pure + (1 - 1/n)/100`. For a fixed pure ratio, larger `n` (up to a point) gives a strictly better full score. Since the ratio `log|A-A|/log|A+A|` for Bose-Chowla sets *decreases* slowly with p while `(1-1/n)/100` *increases*, the optimal p is not necessarily the largest one — but ranking purely by `pure` biases the top-K toward tiny sets. I change the ranking key to `(full, pure)` so the bank prefers sets that actually score well on the objective, and I add a direct "ratio × size bonus" ordering by `full`.

3. **Greedy growth is off-by-one in its deadline check**: `_grow_by_difference` is called with the *global* deadline, so it will consume the entire remaining budget in one shot. I cap it to a sub-budget so `_refine_burst` gets another shot on the result.

These changes are conservative and surgical: they don't change the search operators, only the time budget discipline and the seed-selection criterion, which together should push the score toward the best-known 1.28.
